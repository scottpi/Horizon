# Evaluation goal

Evaluate scientific and research news purely on surprise and consequence for human knowledge — not on professional relevance to any particular reader, and not on proximity to computing or IT.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Paradigm-shifting.** Findings that overturn or substantially revise established understanding, landmark experimental confirmations, or discoveries with consequences reaching across a field or beyond it.
- **7-8: Major.** Significant, well-evidenced results that meaningfully advance a field, unexpected findings with credible support, or high-impact publications with clear methodology.
- **5-6: Noteworthy.** Solid incremental research, interesting findings with modest scope, or well-explained science journalism about ongoing work.
- **3-4: Minor.** Routine incremental results, weakly supported claims, narrow-scope findings, or largely explanatory content without a new result.
- **0-2: Noise.** Press-release hype, unsupported claims, trivial studies, or routine work dressed up as a breakthrough.

# Evaluation guidance

Judge importance by how surprising a finding is relative to prior understanding and how far its consequences reach — across disciplines, populations, or timescales — not by how directly it affects software engineering, IT, or any reader's day job. A finding in astrophysics, biology, or materials science that reshapes scientific understanding outranks a routine computing-adjacent result; relevance to computing or AI earns no bonus points here. Prefer peer-reviewed or well-sourced findings over preliminary or unreviewed claims. Do not equate media attention or a sensational headline with actual scientific importance.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
